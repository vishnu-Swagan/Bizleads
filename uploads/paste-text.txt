2. Product thesis: rank the likelihood to buy, not merely website weakness

A poor or missing website is not automatically a good lead. BizLeads must separate the following concepts and never hide them inside one unexplained score:

1.	Need Score, 0–100 How deficient the current website and digital presence are.
2.	Buyability Score, 0–100 Evidence that the business is active, commercially viable, growing, investing, and plausibly able to buy.
3.	Timing Score, 0–100 Evidence that this is a good moment to approach: a new location, review acceleration, recent business launch, site outage, expired certificate, platform end-of-life, active advertising with a weak landing experience, a recent rebrand, hiring, or another permitted change signal.
4.	Reachability and Eligibility Score, 0–100 Availability and confidence of lawful contact routes, decision-maker relevance where licensed data exists, suppression status, jurisdiction, and outreach saturation.
5.	Agency Fit Score, 0–100 Match to the subscriber’s services, technical stack, portfolio, price range, geography, preferred verticals, delivery capacity, and case studies.
6.	Evidence Confidence, 0–100 Source agreement, freshness, completeness, and whether the claim is measured, owner-confirmed, provider-supplied, or inferred by AI.

Calculate an explainable default priority score:

base = 0.30 × Need + 0.25 × Buyability + 0.20 × Timing + 0.15 × Reachability + 0.10 × AgencyFit

confidenceMultiplier = 0.65 + 0.35 × (EvidenceConfidence / 100)

PriorityScore = clamp(round(base × confidenceMultiplier − RiskPenalty), 0, 100)

RiskPenalty is 0–20 and covers contradictory identity data, closed or duplicate listings, franchise/corporate control, legal or suppression restrictions, unreachable contacts, recently rebuilt high-quality sites, and other “do not pitch” reasons.

All weights must be versioned, testable, and configurable at platform level. Pro and Agency workspaces may adjust bounded weights. Every score must have:

•	A visible breakdown
•	The facts that affected it
•	Source and last-verified timestamp
•	Confidence
•	Score-version identifier
•	Missing-data explanation
•	A “Why this score?” drawer

Apply a Service Lens rather than assuming every subscriber sells the same thing. The same business may be a high-fit lead for a booking-system specialist, a medium-fit lead for a Shopify studio, and a low-fit lead for an accessibility consultancy. Recalculate Fit, recommended scope, and expected value for the selected service without changing the underlying evidence.

Never encode unknown, not checked, not applicable, or provider unavailable as zero. In particular, distinguish:

•	No website
•	Invalid or parked website
•	Website temporarily unavailable
•	Weak website
•	Healthy website
•	Website status unknown

Do the same for social presence. Only score recency, engagement, or profile completeness when those facts come from approved APIs, licensed providers, the business’s own website, or owner-confirmed data. Do not scrape protected social networks or invent follower/activity numbers.

3. Expected Agency Value

After a workspace has enough outcome data, rank leads using the subscriber’s economics:

Expected Agency Value = P(reply) × P(meeting | reply) × P(close | meeting) × expected gross profit − expected acquisition cost

During cold start, use transparent rules-based priors and wide confidence ranges. Do not pretend that probability estimates are accurate before enough outcomes exist. Show “insufficient workspace data” when appropriate.

The user’s onboarding and settings must capture:
